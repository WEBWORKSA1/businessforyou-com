How this calculator works

Most businesses that sell for under a few million dollars are priced on seller's discretionary earnings, the total cash benefit one owner-operator receives. The calculator rebuilds it as:

SDE = net profit before tax + owner salary and payroll taxes + owner perks + interest + depreciation and amortization + documented one-time expenses

It then sets a starting multiple by size. Smaller businesses carry more risk and trade lower: BizBuySell's Q3 2026 Insight Report put the average cash-flow multiple at 2.30x for deals under $1 million and 3.73x for deals above $1 million. Industry, owner dependence, customer concentration, recurring revenue, trend and record quality each move the multiple up or down.

Ranges are planning assumptions calibrated to published market averages. Your actual multiple depends on verified financials and buyer demand.

Why inventory and real estate are added separately

Cash-flow multiples normally cover goodwill plus the equipment needed to run the business. Inventory is usually sold at cost on top of the price, and real estate is valued on its own, then added. Treat them separately in any offer so you do not pay twice.

What the result cannot tell you

  • Whether the add-backs survive due diligence. A buyer's lender will reject anything it cannot verify.
  • Working-capital needs. A business that needs heavy receivables or inventory to run will trade lower.
  • Local demand. Some industries and towns have more buyers than others.
  • Deal terms. Seller financing, an earn-out or a long transition can justify a higher headline price.
