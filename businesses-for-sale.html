How to read a business-for-sale listing

Asking price is what the seller hopes to get, not what the business is worth. Across U.S. small-business deals, sellers typically accept somewhat less than asking, so treat it as an opening position.

Cash flow, usually reported as seller's discretionary earnings (SDE), is pre-tax profit plus the owner's salary, benefits, interest, depreciation and one-off costs. It is the cash one owner-operator could take out before debt payments.

Multiple is asking price divided by cash flow. Most owner-run businesses sell for roughly two to four times SDE. A higher multiple needs a reason: contracted revenue, growth, a manager in place or valuable real estate.

Revenue shows scale. Compare it with cash flow to get the margin; a thin margin leaves little room if costs rise.

Inventory, FF&E and real estate may or may not be included in the price. Always ask, and get them in writing in the letter of intent.
