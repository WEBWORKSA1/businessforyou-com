What buyers pay more for

The multiple a buyer offers moves with risk. These are the factors that move it most in small-business deals.

Books a lender can trust

Tax returns that match your profit-and-loss statements, monthly figures for three years and documented add-backs.

A business that runs without you

A manager or senior staff who quote, sell and solve problems. Written processes for the rest.

Revenue that repeats

Service contracts, memberships, subscriptions and long-standing accounts are worth more than one-off sales.

No customer above 15–20%

Concentration makes buyers and lenders nervous. Spread it before you sell, or expect an earn-out.

A secure location

A long lease or an option to renew, plus landlord consent to assignment, protects the buyer's investment.

Steady or rising trend

Two flat-to-growing years beat one great year. Buyers price the trend, not the peak.

Selling questions

How long does it take to sell a small business?

Many Main Street sales take six to twelve months from preparation to closing. Clean books, a realistic price and a business that runs without the owner shorten that.

Should I use a broker or sell it myself?

Brokers bring buyer networks, pricing experience and deal management, usually for a success fee. Selling yourself saves the fee but costs time and confidentiality. Our broker guide compares both.

How do you keep my sale confidential?

Valuation requests never ask for your business name. Listings are anonymous by default, and buyers request details through us, so your contact information is shared only when you approve.

What documents will buyers ask for?

Three years of tax returns and financial statements, year-to-date figures, your lease, key contracts, an equipment list and staff roster. The due diligence checklist shows the full list from the buyer's side.
