Prepare your business for sale: a 12-month plan

The year before a sale is when an owner can add the most value for the least money. Every month below targets something buyers, and their lenders, will measure.

BusinessForYou.com Editorial Team. Updated November 2026. About 7 minutes to read.

Why preparation pays

A business is priced as a multiple of its cash flow, and the multiple reflects risk. Most preparation work lowers risk in a buyer's eyes: records that can be verified, a team that runs without you, revenue that does not hinge on one client. Lifting the multiple by even half a turn on $200,000 of cash flow adds $100,000 to the price.

Months 12 to 10: get your number and your books

  • Get a baseline valuation range with the calculator or a .
  • Ask your accountant to prepare monthly statements that reconcile to your tax returns and bank accounts.
  • Stop running personal expenses through the business, or document each one so it can be added back.
  • Collect three years of returns, statements and monthly revenue in one place.

Months 9 to 7: reduce owner dependence

  • List every task you do in a typical week. Delegate or document each one.
  • Promote or hire a manager or lead who can quote, sell and solve problems.
  • Introduce staff to key customers and suppliers so relationships are not only with you.
  • Take a two-week holiday and see what breaks. Fix it.

Months 6 to 4: lock in revenue and location

  • Convert regular customers to written agreements or memberships where you can.
  • If one customer is above 15–20% of revenue, grow others or extend that contract.
  • Negotiate a lease extension or renewal option and ask the landlord about assignment.
  • Check licences, permits and key contracts for change-of-control clauses.

Months 3 to 1: package the business

  • Fix visible deferred maintenance; buyers discount for it heavily.
  • Prepare a data room with the documents on the due diligence checklist.
  • Write a one-page summary: what the business does, why customers buy, where it can grow, why you are selling.
  • Choose your route: broker, direct buyer introductions or a free listing.
  • Talk to your accountant about the tax effect of different deal structures.

The preparation scorecard

FactorWeakStrong
RecordsTax returns and books disagreeReconciled monthly statements, three years
Owner roleOwner does sales and operationsManager runs day to day
CustomersOne client over 25%No client over 15%
Revenue typeOne-off jobsContracts, memberships, subscriptions
LocationLease ends within two yearsLong lease or renewal option, assignable
TrendDecliningTwo or more years of steady growth

Keep it confidential

Preparation does not require announcing a sale. Frame the changes as growth and resilience work, which is exactly what they are. Tell staff about a sale only when it is close and you have a plan for their roles.

General information only. Talk to your accountant and attorney before restructuring contracts or ownership.
