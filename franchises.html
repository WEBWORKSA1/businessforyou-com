Buy a franchise, or buy one that is already open

A franchise gives you a tested system and a brand. A franchise resale adds something more valuable: real customers and a track record you can verify. Here is how to choose, and what to check either way.

New unit or resale?

New unitResale
Revenue on day oneNone; ramp-up periodExisting customers and staff
Financial historyFranchisor estimates onlyUnit's own tax returns and statements
LocationYou choose within territory rulesAlready proven, lease must transfer
PriceInitial fee plus build-outUsually a multiple of cash flow
FinancingBased on projectionsBased on actual cash flow
Main riskSlow ramp-upInheriting a weak unit or a short remaining term

Franchise sectors by typical investment

Ranges vary widely by brand and location. Always confirm a franchisor's own estimate in Item 7 of its disclosure document.

Lower investment

Home and commercial cleaning, mobile services, lawn care, handyman and many B2B service brands. Often home-based with few fixed assets.

Mid-range

Fitness and boutique studios, tutoring and kids' education, pet grooming and care, senior home care. Usually a leased unit and a small team.

Higher investment

Quick-service food, cafes, childcare centres and medical or wellness clinics. Build-out, equipment and staffing push costs up.

Largest

Full-service restaurants, hotels and multi-unit area development deals. Typically for experienced operators or investor groups.

The disclosure document items worth reading twice

In the United States, franchisors must give prospective franchisees a Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD) before any agreement is signed. Other countries have similar rules. These sections answer most of the questions that matter:

ItemWhat it tells you
Items 5 and 6Initial franchise fee and ongoing fees: royalties, marketing funds, technology and training charges.
Item 7The franchisor's estimate of your total initial investment, including working capital.
Item 11Training and support the franchisor provides before and after opening.
Item 12Territory: whether you get protection from other units nearby.
Item 17Renewal, termination and transfer rules. Critical for resales.
Item 19Financial performance representations, if the franchisor makes any.
Item 20Openings, closures and transfers by year, plus contacts for current and former franchisees.
Item 21The franchisor's audited financial statements.

Questions to ask current franchisees

  • How long did it take to break even, and what did you spend that you did not expect?
  • Is the support you get worth the royalty you pay?
  • How does the franchisor handle disagreements and territory issues?
  • Knowing what you know now, would you buy again?
  • Which units are struggling, and why?

Extra checks for a resale

  • How many years remain on the franchise agreement, and what does renewal cost?
  • Will the franchisor approve you, and what transfer fee and training apply?
  • Is the unit required to remodel or upgrade soon?
  • Do the unit's own tax returns support the asking price?
